# 02 — GOALS AND STANDARDS THAT SURVIVE A FLARE

Required reading before Phase 4 and Phase 5.

Everything in this file exists because of one fact: **the client filling this out is in pain right now.** A goal framework built for someone whose only obstacle is discipline will quietly hurt this person. Three specific ways, and three specific fixes.

---

## PART 1 — WHY PAIN GOALS BACKFIRE

Left alone, every client will hand you some version of:

- "Be pain-free in 90 days."
- "Get my pain under a 3."
- "Wake up without my back screaming."

It's the most honest thing they'll say all session. It's also the worst possible goal, for two reasons you should be able to explain in plain language:

**1. It's not under their control.** Pain fluctuates for reasons that have nothing to do with effort — sleep, stress, weather, a hard week, a bad chair. Set an outcome they can't steer and you've built a system that reports "failure" on days they did everything right. Do that four or five times and they stop reading the document.

**2. Tracking pain makes pain worse.** Checking your pain level is a scanning behavior. Scanning means the system is watching for threat, and a system watching for threat turns the volume up on what it finds. Setting "pain under a 3" as the goal installs a daily threat check and calls it accountability.

They're not wrong to want out of pain. That's the whole point of the program. It's just not a *goal* — it's the **outcome**, and it arrives as a byproduct of capacity going up. The goals go on the things that actually drive it.

---

## PART 2 — THE CONVERSION

Three kinds of goals are allowed in the Health set. Every health goal must be one of them.

### Capacity — what the body can do
- Walk 45 minutes without needing to sit
- Carry both kids up the stairs
- Deadlift 185 with clean setup and no bracing for impact
- Sit through a 2-hour flight and stand up normal
- Sleep 7+ hours, five nights out of seven

### Behavior — what they actually do
- Move before 8am, six days a week
- Down-regulation practice daily, ten minutes
- Strength train three times a week
- Protein at breakfast, every day

### Participation — the life pain took
- Back on the Sunday ride
- Coach the season without sitting out
- Play a full round of golf
- Get on the floor with the grandkids and get back up

**All three are things they control. All three go up as the nervous system settles. None of them require a single pain rating.**

### The redirect script

When they hand you a pain goal — and they will — don't just swap it silently. Say this:

> Hold on, I want to change one thing about that, and here's why.
>
> "Pain-free in 90 days" isn't a goal, it's a weather forecast. It moves for reasons that have nothing to do with how well you're doing this — you can do everything right and wake up sore because you slept funny. Score yourself against that and you'll be "failing" on days you crushed it.
>
> The other problem is quieter. Tracking your pain means checking your pain, every day, on purpose. That's a threat scan. And a system that's scanning for threat turns the volume *up* on what it finds. We'd be building the exact loop we're trying to get you out of.
>
> Getting out of pain is the point — it's just not the target. It's what happens on the way to something else. So tell me the something else.
>
> What would you be doing, specifically, if the pain weren't in the way?

Whatever comes back next is the real goal. Write that one down.

### If they resist
Some people want the number. Don't fight it:

> Fair. Keep it for yourself if it helps — just keep it off the lock screen and out of the affirmations. Those are the things you'll read every morning, and I don't want your first act of the day to be checking whether it hurts.

## PART 3 — EXTERNAL STRUCTURE

A standard nobody can see is a wish with better grammar.

At 5am, in the dark, with a body that hurts, the client will renegotiate — and they'll win, because they are smart and they will have an excellent reason. External structure means the renegotiation costs something visible.

Go standard by standard and ask: **"Who knows if you don't do it?"**

If the answer is "nobody," build one. In rough order of strength:

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| A person who is waiting | Training partner, a walk with a neighbor, a standing call |
| A booked slot with a cost | Class with a cancellation fee, a scheduled appointment |
| A visible log someone sees | Weekly check-in with Brandon, a shared tracker |
| A public commitment | Told the group, posted in the community |
| A recurring calendar block with an alarm | Weakest — last resort, not first |

One good structure beats four weak ones. Don't turn this into a compliance apparatus — two or three externally-bound standards is a strong week.

### Close the gray zone
For each standard, get **counts / doesn't count** explicitly:

> What actually counts as hitting this? And what's the version that *feels* like hitting it but isn't?

They will find the loopholes in a vague standard — everyone does, it's not a character defect, it's just what a tired brain does at 5am. Close them now, while they're clear-headed. Example: *"Moving my body" counts as a 20-minute walk or better. It does not count as walking to the car, or "being on my feet at work all day."*

## PART 4 — THE FLARE-DAY MINIMUM

**This is the highest-leverage item in the entire document. Do not let a client leave Phase 4 without it.**

The predictable failure pattern in this population is not laziness. It's this:

1. Things go well. They feel good.
2. Feeling good, they do too much.
3. Flare.
4. Everything on the list breaks at once.
5. Restarting means admitting the streak is dead, so restarting feels worse than not restarting.
6. They quit — and they conclude their body failed, which reinforces the exact belief they dismantled in Phase 2.

**Step 5 is where you intervene, and you do it in advance.** A flare-day minimum means the streak never actually breaks. There's no restart to dread, because they never stopped.

For every standard, capture a floor version:

| Standard | Flare-day minimum |
|---|---|
| Move before 8am, six days a week | Five minutes of the drills that calm things down. Still counts. |
| Strength train 3x/week | Show up, do the warm-up, leave. Still counts. |
| In bed by 10:30 | In bed by 10:30. This one doesn't change — it's what a flare day needs most. |
| Coffee-and-journal, first hour | Three lines. Still counts. |

Frame it plainly:

> These aren't the fallback. They're the standard on that day. You didn't miss — you did the flare-day version, and that *is* the thing. The streak stays alive.
>
> The people who get through this program aren't the ones who never have a bad week. They're the ones who already decided what a bad week looks like before it showed up.

Some standards shouldn't scale down — sleep and downregulation usually get *more* important on a flare day, not less. Say so when it comes up.

---

## PART 5 — QUALITY GATE

Before assembling the final document, check:

- [ ] No goal is a pain rating, a pain-free date, or a symptom score
- [ ] Every health goal is capacity, behavior, or participation
- [ ] Every goal is something they control
- [ ] At least two standards have real external structure — a person or a cost, not just a calendar alert
- [ ] Every standard has counts / doesn't count defined
- [ ] Every standard has a flare-day minimum
- [ ] No affirmation asserts the absence of a symptom

If something's missing, go back and ask. Don't fill it in yourself — Hard Rule 2.
