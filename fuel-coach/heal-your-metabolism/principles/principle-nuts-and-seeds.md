# Nuts and Seeds

> **Type:** Principle  
> **Topics:** nuts, seeds, PUFA ranking, oxalates, phytates, soaking  
> Source: Kate Deering, *How to Heal Your Metabolism* (2015). These are the author's positions (influenced by Ray Peat), not mainstream consensus. Present them as "the book recommends...".  
> **Refer out:** Clients with a history of kidney stones should follow their provider's oxalate guidance.

- The book calls them anti-metabolic. Its reasons:
  - high PUFA (walnuts worst)
  - high oxalates: **42–469 mg/100 g**. Roasted almonds are highest; raw macadamia lowest.
  - phytates, which also block minerals from the *other* foods eaten at the same time
  - lectins and trypsin inhibitors
  - 25 g of protein from nuts costs about 600 kcal
- Raw nuts are hard to digest. Oil-roasted nuts add PUFA. Dry-roasted is a compromise.
- **PUFA ranking, worst → best:**
  1. walnuts
  2. sunflower seeds
  3. flax seeds
  4. chia seeds
  5. sesame seeds
  6. pecans
  7. Brazil nuts
  8. pistachios
  9. almonds
  10. hazelnuts
  11. cashews
  12. macadamia nuts
- **If a client still wants nuts:**
  1. Eat organic.
  2. Soak or sprout them, to reduce trypsin inhibitors, phytates and lectins.
  3. Eat in moderation: 1–2×/week, not daily (Ch. 11). Appendix B says 1–2×/month while healing.
  4. Choose the lowest-PUFA nuts: hazelnuts, macadamias, cashews.
- **Snack swap:** Parmesan or ricotta + fruit instead of nuts.
- Nuts are not a protein source.
