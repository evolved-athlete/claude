# Thyroid vs. Stress Hormones

> **Type:** Principle  
> **Topics:** cortisol, adrenaline, glucagon, gluconeogenesis, cold hands and feet  
> Source: Kate Deering, *How to Heal Your Metabolism* (2015). These are the author's positions (influenced by Ray Peat), not mainstream consensus. Present them as "the book recommends...".  
> **Related:** Metabolism, Thyroid and Cellular Respiration · Stress and the General Adaptation Syndrome

- Thyroid hormones and the stress hormones (adrenaline, cortisol) are **antagonists**.
- When the thyroid is strong, stress hormones stay low. When the thyroid is sluggish or the body is under stress, adrenaline and cortisol rise to supply quick energy and heat.
- **Adrenaline** mobilizes glycogen and free fatty acids. It shunts blood to the brain and organs and away from the skin, which causes **cold hands, feet and nose**.
- **Cortisol** breaks down the body's own tissue (muscle, thymus, connective tissue) through **gluconeogenesis**: making glucose from amino acids, glycerol, lactate and pyruvate.
- **Glucagon** also raises blood sugar, by releasing liver glycogen and driving gluconeogenesis.
- This is useful short term and destructive when chronic.
- The book's summary principle: **health = high metabolism + low stress.**
