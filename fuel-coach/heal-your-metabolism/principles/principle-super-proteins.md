# Super Proteins

> **Type:** Principle  
> **Topics:** milk, eggs, liver, shellfish, white fish, potato protein, bone broth, gelatin, glycine  
> Source: Kate Deering, *How to Heal Your Metabolism* (2015). These are the author's positions (influenced by Ray Peat), not mainstream consensus. Present them as "the book recommends...".  
> **Related:** How Much Protein · Muscle Meats · Beef Bone Broth

**Appendix B frequencies are in bold.**

| Protein | How much / how often | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Milk and dairy** | **2–6 servings/day**. The author has about 4 servings plus other dairy, roughly ⅓ of her protein. | Whole milk has ~8 g protein per cup and ~33 g per quart. Additive-free cheese: Parmigiano-Reggiano, ricotta. See "Why Milk" and "Choosing Milk, Cheese and Yogurt". |
| **Whole eggs** (pastured, organic, soy- and corn-free) | **Up to 2/day** | Eat the yolk. The protein split is 60% white, 40% yolk, but nearly all the nutrients are in the yolk. Pair with OJ or fruit, because eggs strongly stimulate insulin. Conventional eggs only: limit to **2–3 per week**. |
| **Grass-fed beef liver** | **3–6 oz once every 7–10 days** | 25 g protein per 4 oz; very high in vitamins A and B12, plus copper, zinc and selenium. "4 oz liver ≈ 5 lb fruit" in nutrients. More is not better, because of its tryptophan. Cook in coconut oil or ghee and eat with cooked fruit or vegetables. |
| **Shellfish**: oysters, shrimp, scallops, mussels, clams, crab, lobster | **1–3×/week** (1–2× in Ch. 8) | Zinc, copper, selenium, B12. **Always cook** (the main foodborne-illness risk is raw shellfish). The FDA allows up to 12 oz/week. Cook in butter and serve with cooked vegetables or fruit. |
| **Warm-water / low-fat white fish**: sole, cod, whitefish, tilapia, flounder, catfish | **2–4×/week** | Low in PUFA. **Fatty fish** (salmon, mackerel, herring, albacore) only **1–2×/month**, fried in butter or coconut oil. |
| **Potato protein** | — | 2 lb well-cooked potato ≈ the protein of 1 L milk (~33 g). Juiced potato protein suits a very compromised metabolism or vegetarians. See recipe "Eggshell Calcium". |
| **Bone broth** | **1–3 cups/day** (healthy 1–2; hypometabolic 3–4+) | ~8 g protein per cup. **Always 1 cup with any muscle-meat meal.** |
| **Powdered gelatin** (Great Lakes) | **1–6 Tbsp/day** | Add to OJ, coffee, milk, smoothies, soups and sauces. |

**Share of protein from broth + gelatin:** healthy **25–35%**; hypometabolic or poor digestion **up to 50%**.

**Gelatin types**
- **Bovine or porcine (regular):** thickens. Dissolve it in warm liquid first. Use for gummies, custards, soups and sauces.
- **Hydrolyzed (collagen hydrolysate):** dissolves in cold liquid and **does not thicken**. Best for OJ, smoothies, coffee and workout drinks.
- Gelatin is **not a complete protein** and not ideal for muscle building. For muscle gain, use dairy, eggs, fish, shellfish and meat.

**Benefits the book links to gelatin:**
- improved digestion, more gastric juices, and healing of the gut lining (ulcer protection)
- less joint inflammation; help with arthritis
- better sleep and memory
- better insulin sensitivity and steadier blood glucose; help with diabetes
- muscle sparing
- fewer allergies
- better wound healing and liver detox
- healthier skin, hair and nails

**Glycine** (abundant in gelatin) is used for:
- glutathione, creatine and bile salts
- liver recovery
- gastric acid secretion
- sleep, memory and calm (it is an inhibitory neurotransmitter)

Get it from food, not isolated glycine supplements.
