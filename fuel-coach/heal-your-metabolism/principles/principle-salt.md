# Salt

> **Type:** Principle  
> **Topics:** salt, sodium, blood pressure, RAAS, Morton canning salt  
> Source: Kate Deering, *How to Heal Your Metabolism* (2015). These are the author's positions (influenced by Ray Peat), not mainstream consensus. Present them as "the book recommends...".  
> **Refer out:** Clients with hypertension, kidney disease or heart failure must clear salt changes with their provider.

- **Claimed benefits:**
  - reduces inflammation and cramping
  - helps magnesium absorption
  - suppresses aldosterone and supports the thyroid
  - improves insulin sensitivity
  - raises metabolic rate and body heat
  - relieves constipation
  - improves sleep
  - helps wound healing
- **The RAAS mechanism, as the book explains it:**
  - Three things switch on the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system: **low blood pressure** (below about 90/60), **stress** (sympathetic activation), and **low salt**. High salt does not.
  - Angiotensin II then raises blood pressure four ways:
    1. constricts arteries
    2. releases ADH, so the body retains water
    3. makes the kidneys reabsorb sodium
    4. releases aldosterone, which retains sodium, loses potassium and causes edema
  - The system normally switches itself off. Chronic stress keeps it on, which the book calls the real driver of hypertension.
- **The book's argument against restriction:**
  - Low salt raises noradrenaline and activates the RAAS.
  - Studies cited: Alderman (1995 and 1998), a Belgian study (2011), and NEJM 2014 with more than 100,000 people.
  - In the NEJM study, **3,000–6,000 mg sodium/day** had the lowest death and CVD rates. **Under 3,000 mg** had about 27% higher risk.
- **How much:** salt food **to taste**; it shouldn't taste overly salty.
  - Add a pinch to OJ, milk, gelatin drinks and workout drinks.
  - Increase **slowly**. Too fast causes water retention and bloating.
- **Who:**
  - People already eating whole food, with good habits, benefit.
  - People eating mostly processed food (already high in salt) should clean up the diet first.
  - **Refer clients with hypertension or kidney disease to their provider.**
- **Type:** clean **white** salt, not pink or gray. The book recommends **Morton's Canning & Pickling Salt** (non-iodized).
