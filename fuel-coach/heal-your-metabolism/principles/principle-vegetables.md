# Vegetables: Which, and How to Prepare Them

> **Type:** Principle  
> **Topics:** vegetables, cruciferous, goitrogens, cooking vegetables, salads, root vegetables  
> Source: Kate Deering, *How to Heal Your Metabolism* (2015). These are the author's positions (influenced by Ray Peat), not mainstream consensus. Present them as "the book recommends...".  
> **Related:** The Fiber Argument · Raw Carrot Protocol

- **Above-ground and leafy vegetables carry plant defenses:**
  - oxalates, which block calcium (spinach and kale are poor calcium sources)
  - lectins
  - lignans (phytoestrogens; worst in flax, sesame and cruciferous vegetables)
  - **goitrogens** (cruciferous vegetables, soy)
  - pyridoxine glucoside, which cuts B6 availability 75–85%
  - pectin and cellulose, which feed bacteria and raise endotoxin and serotonin in a damaged gut
- **Root vegetables** grow below ground and carry fewer defenses.

## Rules
- **Cook vegetables (and fruit) well.**
  - Cooking breaks down pectin, lignin, lectins and goitrogens.
  - **Cook cruciferous vegetables about 30 minutes** to reduce the goitrogenic effect.
  - Raw carrot is the exception (see "Raw Carrot Protocol").
- **Always eat with fat and protein**, preferably butter or coconut oil.
- **Best vegetables:**
  - roots: potato, light sweet potato, beets, turnips, carrots
  - "fruit-vegetables": squash, zucchini, pumpkin, tomato, cucumber, peppers
- **Salads:** the author went from salads 2–3×/day to **2–3×/month**. A gut-friendly salad is **raw carrot, tomato, cucumber, onion and peppers** with olive oil and vinegar.
- **Who should consider cutting leafy and cruciferous greens:** people with digestive issues, gas or bloating, sugar or salt cravings, or allergies.
