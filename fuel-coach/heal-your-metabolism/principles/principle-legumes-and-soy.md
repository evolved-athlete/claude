# Legumes and Soy

> **Type:** Principle  
> **Topics:** beans, soaking beans, soy, tofu, tempeh, isoflavones  
> Source: Kate Deering, *How to Heal Your Metabolism* (2015). These are the author's positions (influenced by Ray Peat), not mainstream consensus. Present them as "the book recommends...".  
> **Related:** Estrogen Dominance · Raw Carrot Protocol

**Legumes**
- They are 10–25% protein but contain lectins, phytates, trypsin inhibitors, oxalates and PUFA.
- Least problematic: **cooked fresh green beans and peas**. Hardest: kidney, lima and black beans.
- Canned beans, even organic, are not soaked.
- **Preparation:**
  1. Soak **1 cup of beans in 5 cups of warm water overnight**.
  2. Discard the soaking water.
  3. Cook thoroughly.
  4. Eat with butter, ghee or coconut oil.
- Soaking or sprouting cuts phytates 40–70%. Cooking alone leaves about 92%.
- **Frequency while healing: 1–2×/month at most**, or avoid.

**Soy** is the book's "most degraded legume."
- **Goitrogens** block iodine uptake.
- **Isoflavones** are phytoestrogens that inhibit thyroid peroxidase.
- It is very high in phytates and contains trypsin inhibitors and hemagglutinin.
- About 95% of US soy is genetically modified.
- The FDA suggests 25 g/day of soy. By comparison, Asians eat about 3–8 g/day of fermented soy.
- **Avoid:**
  - soy milk, soy cheese, soy burgers and soy sausage
  - tofu and edamame
  - soy protein, soy lecithin, soy oil and soy flour
  - soy bars, soy snacks and soy creamer
- **Allowed sparingly:** non-GMO fermented soy (tempeh, miso, natto, gluten-free soy sauce), e.g. tempeh 1–2×/month.

|---|---|
