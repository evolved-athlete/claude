# Coach Guardrails for This Source

> **Type:** Coaching rules  
> **Topics:** how to use, attribution, medical referral, pacing  
> Source: Kate Deering, *How to Heal Your Metabolism* (2015). These are the author's positions (influenced by Ray Peat), not mainstream consensus. Present them as "the book recommends...".

- **Attribute everything to the book.** These are Deering's (and Ray Peat's) positions, not settled consensus. Phrase them as "This approach holds that…" or "Deering recommends…".
- **Several positions go against mainstream nutrition guidance.** Present these as the book's view and don't overstate the evidence:
  - PUFAs, including fish oil and omega-3s, are harmful.
  - Leafy and cruciferous vegetables should be limited.
  - Fiber is often a problem.
  - More salt is protective.
  - White sugar and juice are good fuels.
  - Grains should be avoided while healing.
- **Refer to a medical provider** before a client changes anything involving:
  - medications (statins, thyroid medication, birth control, blood-pressure drugs)
  - salt intake with hypertension or kidney disease
  - diabetes or blood-sugar medication
  - pregnancy
  - kidney stones
  - eating-disorder history
  - large calorie increases in a medically complex client
- **Go slowly.** This is the book's own #1 rule: make one or two changes at a time. Adding food, sugar, salt or fat too fast causes weight gain, water retention or digestive upset.
