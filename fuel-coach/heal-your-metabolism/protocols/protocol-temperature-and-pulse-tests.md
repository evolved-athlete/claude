# Temperature and Pulse Tests

> **Type:** Protocol  
> **Topics:** basal temperature, pulse, post-meal check, post-workout check  
> Source: Kate Deering, *How to Heal Your Metabolism* (2015). These are the author's positions (influenced by Ray Peat), not mainstream consensus. Present them as "the book recommends...".  
> **Refer out:** These readings are feedback, not diagnosis. Persistent symptoms belong with a provider.  
> **Related:** Daily Log · Signs of Healthy vs. Slow Metabolism

**Basal temperature (Barnes protocol)**
1. Shake down a thermometer and leave it by the bed.
2. On waking, before moving, place it **in the armpit and lie still for 10 minutes**.
3. A healthy waking reading is **≥ 97.8 °F (36.6 °C)**.
4. Temperature should rise through the day to about **98.6 °F (37 °C) around midday**.

- A mercury thermometer is most accurate. An oral digital thermometer is acceptable; take several readings.
- **Falsely high readings:** fever, electric blanket, hot weather, hot drinks, **high cortisol or adrenaline**. A stressed person can read warm.
- **Falsely low readings:** starvation, a cold, cold drinks, cool weather, pituitary or adrenal problems.

**Pulse**
- Ideal resting pulse is **75–90 bpm**.
- A low pulse means fitness, not health. Endurance athletes often run **mid-40s to mid-50s bpm and 94.5–96 °F**, which the book calls adaptation to chronic stress.
- Blood-pressure problems, heart conditions, diabetes and cancer can change pulse. Use pulse together with temperature and symptoms.

**Post-meal check**
- Measure temperature and pulse **on waking** and again **20–30 minutes after each meal**.
- A supportive meal should raise both, and the person should feel **warm, alert and content**.
- **How to read post-meal changes in a stressed or hypometabolic person:**

| Before the meal | After the meal | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Warm | Temperature and pulse **fall** | The earlier warmth came from adrenaline and cortisol. Sugar and salt lowered the stress hormones, so readings dropped. |
| — | Temperature **unchanged** | The meal was probably too small or had the wrong macro ratio. |
| Pulse already **>100** | Pulse rises further | Probably too much protein relative to carbs (e.g. eggs alone). That raises insulin, then adrenaline. |
| Pulse **<70** | Pulse rises | Good meal. Thyroid output may have increased. |
| Pulse **>100**, temperature **<97.8 °F** | Temperature rises, pulse falls | Good meal. Stress hormones dropped and heat production rose. |
| Pulse **<70**, temperature **<97.8 °F** | Both rise | Good meal. |

These readings are feedback, not exact science.

**Post-workout check**
- If temperature and pulse drop after training, the workout suppressed the thyroid: it was too long, too hard or too intense.
- Fix: shorten the session, rest more, or lower the intensity.
